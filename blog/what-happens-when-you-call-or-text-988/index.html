What Happens When You Call or Text 988?

The short answer: When you call 988, you hear a short greeting and a menu, then hold music, then a trained crisis counselor who asks if you are safe and listens. Texts and chats follow the same pattern after a few written prompts. The service is free and confidential, and the 988 Lifeline says less than two percent of calls involve emergency services. You can contact 988 for yourself or for someone you are worried about.

What happens in the first minute of a 988 call?

The 988 Lifeline's own walkthrough describes four steps for a phone call. First, a recorded message offers service options. Then you hear hold music. Then a counselor says hello and asks if you are safe. Then the real conversation starts.

The menu options are:

  • Press 1 for the Veterans Crisis Line, which serves veterans, service members, and their families.
  • Press 2 for a Spanish-speaking counselor.
  • Press 3 for the LGBTQ+ youth specialized service. This option closed in July 2025. It came back on September 30, 2026, according to a KFF policy analysis, and the 988 Lifeline site now states that it "has been restored pursuant to funding and congressional direction."
  • Stay on the line to reach your local 988 crisis center.

You do not have to listen to the whole menu. The SAMHSA 988 FAQ says you can bypass the options by dialing zero. If you speak a language other than English or Spanish, the Lifeline uses an interpreter service that covers more than 240 additional languages, at no cost to you.

People who are Deaf or hard of hearing have options too. The 988 Lifeline FAQ says TTY users can use their preferred relay service or dial 711 and then 988. There is also an ASL videophone service.

What does the counselor actually do?

Every call, text, and chat starts the same way. The counselor introduces themselves and asks about your safety. After that, they listen and try to understand how the problem is affecting you. SAMHSA describes the support as personal and judgment-free, and the counselor may share resources in your area.

You do not need to be suicidal to reach out. SAMHSA calls 988 a hotline for mental health, suicide, and substance use problems, and says "there is no reason too big or too small." People call about panic, grief, a relapse, a fight at home, or a slow slide into hopelessness. If you are working through low mood that has lasted weeks, depression.md covers treatment options you can raise once the crisis has passed.

A typical contact ends with a plan. That might be a safety plan for tonight, a referral to a local clinic, or a follow-up step you agree on together. If the conversation shows you need more care than a hotline can give, the counselor can talk through next steps. Our guide on when inpatient treatment makes sense explains how that decision gets made, including when 988 fits better than the ER.

How is texting or chatting 988 different?

Text and chat add a short intake step before you reach a person. According to the 988 Lifeline, a text to 988 goes like this:

  1. You get options: stay with the main Lifeline, text AYUDA for Spanish, or text the Veterans Crisis Line directly at 838255.
  2. You reply "Next," which accepts the Lifeline's terms of service.
  3. You answer a brief survey so the counselor knows how to help.
  4. You get a "Wait" message while the system connects you.
  5. A counselor says hello and asks if you are safe.
  6. The counselor listens, supports you, and may share resources.

Chat runs through chat.988lifeline.org in English or Spanish. SAMHSA says the pre-chat survey takes less than five minutes. When you click "Start a chat," you accept the terms of service and wait for a counselor. Text and chat can feel easier if you cannot talk out loud, or if someone nearby could overhear.

Where does your call go, and what if no one answers?

Calls go to a local crisis center when possible. The SAMHSA FAQ explains two ways this happens. If your carrier uses georouting, the call goes to a center near your approximate location. If not, it goes to a center tied to the first six digits of your phone number (area code and prefix), wherever you happen to be.

That second method is why someone with an out-of-state number used to reach a center far from home. SAMHSA says the nationwide wireless carriers have now turned on georouting for calls. Smaller carriers have until December 14, 2026.

Texts are behind calls. The FCC compliance guide says nationwide carriers must georoute 988 texts by April 16, 2027, and smaller carriers by October 16, 2028. Until then, many texts still route by area code.

If the local center cannot pick up within a set time, the contact is not dropped. SAMHSA says it rolls over automatically to the national backup network. You still reach a trained counselor. They may just know less about resources in your specific town.

Will 988 send police or an ambulance?

Usually not. The 988 Lifeline says counselors contact 911, police, or a sheriff only when risk of harm to self or others is imminent or in progress, and when a safer plan cannot be worked out with you. By its own figures, less than two percent of 988 Lifeline calls involve emergency services. When they are involved, more than half of those dispatches happen with the caller's consent.

SAMHSA gives examples of when a counselor would bring in 911: a suicide attempt in progress, a specific plan with the means to carry it out right away, or a suspected overdose. Where possible, the Lifeline prefers other options, such as a safety plan, a mobile crisis team, or help getting to a crisis stabilization unit or emergency department.

Two facts matter here. First, 988 does not use geolocation. SAMHSA says pinpoint location for dispatch "is not currently enabled for 988 and is not under active consideration." Second, georouting does not pass your precise location through the network. If you have had a hard experience with emergency responders, you can say so to the counselor. Is 988 confidential?

Yes. The 988 Lifeline says the service is free and the conversation is confidential. Its FAQ says that because of this, it cannot give out information about or records of calls, even to someone asking about a call they or a family member made.

There is one detail people notice in the greeting: calls may be recorded for quality assurance, evaluation, or training. The Lifeline says individual crisis centers in the network may use recordings for those purposes. SAMHSA also notes that the Lifeline administrator, Vibrant Emotional Health, is a nonprofit and does not sell Lifeline data.

You can also contact 988 about someone else. The Lifeline's guide to helping someone else says to reach out any time a friend is struggling and to never keep a friend's plan to hurt themselves a secret. A counselor can coach you on what to say and what is available nearby. If the person will not accept help, our post on helping someone who refuses treatment covers your options.

The bottom line

Calling 988 means a short menu, a brief wait, and a trained counselor who starts by asking if you are safe. Texting and chat add a short survey first. Most contacts end with support and a plan, not a dispatch. The Veterans, Spanish, and restored LGBTQ+ youth options give specialized help, and a national backup answers if your local center cannot. You can reach out for yourself or for someone you love, at any hour.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it free to call or text 988?

Yes. The 988 Lifeline says the service is free. Interpretation in more than 240 additional languages is also offered at no cost to the caller.

Can I skip the menu when I call 988?

Yes. SAMHSA says you can bypass the menu options by dialing zero. If you do not pick an option, you are connected to a local 988 crisis center by default.

Can I reach 988 if I am Deaf or hard of hearing?

Yes. The 988 Lifeline says TTY users can use their preferred relay service or dial 711 and then 988. It also offers an ASL videophone service, and text and chat are open to everyone.

Can 988 track my location?

No. SAMHSA says 988 does not use geolocation and is not considering it. Georouting sends your call to a nearby center using your general area only, and it does not pass your precise location through the network.

How long will I wait for a counselor?

You hear hold music while the system connects you, and wait times vary by center and time of day. If your local center does not answer within a set time, the call rolls over to the national backup network so it is still answered.
